About

What this is.

We live in the space between what we intend and what we achieve. Policies, technologies, organisations and individuals are all judged by outcomes, yet most effort is spent on intentions. The Human Latency explores the hidden systems that shape the journey from one to the other, and how we might narrow the gap.
The founding idea

Intelligence is the distance between intent and outcome.

The Human Latency began with that one sentence, written in the margin of a project review that had gone politely, thoroughly wrong. The plan had been good. The people had been good. The outcome was neither. What sat between them was a set of hidden systems nobody had drawn — and once you start drawing them, you cannot stop. This publication, the Vault, and the client work all come from the same habit: mapping the gap, then quietly narrowing it.

The founder

One person, many filing cabinets.

Based in New Zealand. Two decades inside large programmes and small businesses, watching intent turn into outcome — sometimes gracefully, usually not. Now writing about the gap and building the tools that close it, one plain-text system at a time.

The worldview

Five beliefs, filed.

  1. Outcomes are the only honest measure of a system.
  2. Most failure is friction, not malice.
  3. Human judgement belongs in the gap — design for it, never around it.
  4. Your tools should live on your machine and answer to you.
  5. Small, boring systems outlast grand, fragile ones.
What we're for

We are stubborn about one thing: whether a system actually makes someone's day better. Not whether it reads well in a plan, or reports cleanly, or wins the meeting. A system that works on paper and costs a person their afternoon has failed. If you would like one built properly for your work, this is how we work.

How we work
